Tipping Behavior Analysis

This Project uses SAS software to analyze tipping behavior across factors like gender, smoking status, day of the week, mealtime, and party size. Key findings include that males tend to tip more generously than females, while smoking status shows no significant impact. However, the correlation between total bill amounts and tip percentages is weak, indicating other factors also influence tipping behavior. These insights can help businesses optimize service strategies.

Analysis of The Gambia's Bilateral Trade Flows

This Project applies the gravity model of trade to analyze The Gambia’s bilateral trade flows with its major trading partners. The analysis covers a 20-year period from 2000 to 2019, focusing on trade relationships between The Gambia and 24 key partners. Using Stata, the study employs a panel data framework to account for unobservable heterogeneity and capture the dynamics among variables over time. The Hausman test strongly rejects the null hypothesis of no correlation between the explanatory variables and country-specific effects, leading to the selection of the fixed-effects model over the random-effects model. Empirical results reveal that the GDP, population of partner countries, and a dummy variable for EU membership have positive and significant impacts on The Gambia’s bilateral trade flows. Conversely, per capita GDP differentials and geographical distance exhibit significant negative effects on The Gambia’s total bilateral trade flows. The findings provide robust evidence that the gravity model effectively explains the pattern of The Gambia’s bilateral trade flows, as most variables align with their theoretically expected signs.
